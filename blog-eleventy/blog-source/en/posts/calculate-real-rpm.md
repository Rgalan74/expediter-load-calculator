<p class="text-lg md:text-xl text-gray-200 font-medium mb-6">
  Did you know that many independent carriers lose thousands of dollars a year by accepting seemingly
  well-paying loads? The problem lies in incomplete mathematics that brokers and dispatchers exploit to their
  advantage.
</p>

<h2 class="text-xl md:text-2xl font-bold text-white mt-8 mb-4">📌 The Most Common Calculation Mistake</h2>
<p>Most owner-operators and independent expediters calculate their Rate Per Mile using the simple formula
  provided on the broker's rate confirmation:</p>

<div class="bg-white/5 border border-white/10 rounded-xl p-4 font-mono text-cyan-400 my-4 text-center">
  Offered Pay ÷ Loaded Miles = Apparent RPM
</div>

<div class="callout warning">
  <strong class="text-orange-400 font-bold">⚠️ Why is this a serious error?</strong>
  <p>Because it completely ignores the miles you don't get paid for but that <strong>still cost you</strong>
    fuel, vehicle wear, and time. Your truck doesn't magically teleport to the shipper, nor does it fly back home
    after delivery.</p>
</div>

<h2 class="text-xl md:text-2xl font-bold text-white mt-8 mb-4">✅ The Correct Real RPM Formula</h2>
<p>To understand the true financial health of your trucking business and know if a load actually delivers a net
  profit, you must include all miles in the equation:</p>

<div class="bg-white/5 border border-white/10 rounded-xl p-4 font-mono text-green-400 my-4 text-center">
  REAL RPM = Total Pay ÷ (Deadhead + Loaded + Repositioning)
</div>

<p class="mt-4"><strong>Where:</strong></p>
<ul class="list-disc pl-6 space-y-2 my-4">
  <li><strong>Deadhead:</strong> The empty miles you must drive from your current location to the load pickup
    point.</li>
  <li><strong>Loaded:</strong> The loaded miles for which the broker is paying you to go from point A to point
    B.</li>
  <li><strong>Repositioning (Escape Miles):</strong> The empty miles you must drive from the delivery point back
    to the nearest active freight hub if you deliver to a dead state.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="text-xl md:text-2xl font-bold text-white mt-8 mb-4">📊 Case Study: The Hidden Cost of a Texas Trip</h2>
<p>Let's look at a real load board offer example:</p>

<div class="table-container">
  <ul class="space-y-2">
    <li>📍 <strong>Your Location:</strong> Toledo, OH</li>
    <li>📦 <strong>Pickup:</strong> Detroit, MI (65 miles deadhead)</li>
    <li>🏁 <strong>Delivery:</strong> Laredo, TX (1,000 loaded miles)</li>
    <li>💰 <strong>Offered Pay:</strong> $1,500</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<div class="grid md:grid-cols-2 gap-4 my-6">
  <div class="glass-card p-5 border-red-500/20 border">
    <h3 class="font-bold text-red-400 mb-2">❌ Incorrect Calculation (Broker)</h3>
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-400 mb-3">Only counts loaded miles:</p>
    <div class="font-mono text-lg text-white mb-2">$1,500 ÷ 1,000 mi = <strong class="text-red-400">$1.50/mi</strong></div>
    <p class="text-xs text-red-500">"Great rate of $1.50 per mile!" (Big mistake).</p>
  </div>
  <div class="glass-card p-5 border-green-500/30 border">
    <h3 class="font-bold text-green-400 mb-2">✅ Correct Calculation (SmartLoad)</h3>
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-400 mb-3">All miles included:</p>
    <div class="font-mono text-lg text-white mb-2">$1,500 ÷ 1,345 mi = <strong class="text-green-400">$1.12/mi</strong></div>
    <p class="text-xs text-green-400">Includes 65 deadhead mi and 280 escape mi back to San Antonio.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="callout tip">
  <strong class="text-cyan-400">💡 Financial Impact Analysis</strong>
  <p>If your cargo van's true operational cost (Break-Even) is <strong>$1.10 per mile</strong>, the first
    calculation falsely leads you to believe you're earning a healthy <strong>$0.40/mile</strong> profit. In
    reality, you're only making <strong>$0.02/mile</strong>. This 34% calculation error represents
    <strong>$511 in hidden losses</strong> on a single trip!</p>
</div>

<h2 class="text-xl md:text-2xl font-bold text-white mt-8 mb-4">🏆 How to Avoid State "Traps"</h2>
<p>A trap zone is a destination state (such as Florida, Colorado, Oregon, or deep South Texas) where loads pay
  well to enter, but virtually no freight pays to leave. When delivering to these regions, you must add the
  operational cost of driving empty to a hot hub to get out.</p>

  escape miles, seamlessly factoring repositioning costs into the decision engine to display an instant green or
  red alert before you book.</p>
