<h2 class="text-xl md:text-2xl font-bold text-white mt-8 mb-4">📍 The Geography of Freight: Why Zones Matter</h2>
<p>Unlike 53-foot Semi-trucks that navigate a fairly homogeneous national market, <strong>expediting vans</strong>
  rely heavily on quick industrial loads (<em>just-in-time</em> automotive parts, medical equipment, and
  high-value cargo). This means volume is highly concentrated in heavy manufacturing regions.</p>

<div class="callout warning">
  <strong>❌ The Mistake of Chasing the Sun</strong>
  <p>Many independent owner-operators make the mistake of driving toward beautiful states like Florida, Arizona,
    Nevada, or Colorado, drawn by good inbound rates, only to discover there is no outbound freight. These are
    the infamous <strong>Trap Zones (Blackholes)</strong>, where your van can sit stranded for days or be forced
    to run hundreds of empty repositioning miles (deadhead) to get out.</p>
</div>

<h2 class="text-xl md:text-2xl font-bold text-white mt-8 mb-4">🔥 The Top 5 Hot and Profitable Freight Zones</h2>

<h3 class="text-lg md:text-xl font-semibold text-white mt-6 mb-2">1. The Industrial Midwest Belt (Midwest Core)</h3>
<p><strong>Key states:</strong> Michigan, Ohio, Indiana, and Illinois.</p>
<p>This is the <strong>absolute epicenter</strong> of expediting in the US due to the massive automotive and
  manufacturing industry. Cities like Detroit (MI), Cleveland (OH), Toledo (OH), Fort Wayne (IN), and Chicago
  (IL) offer constant load offers 24/7. Outbound rates here are consistently the highest in the country, and
  reloads book in minutes.</p>

<h3 class="text-lg md:text-xl font-semibold text-white mt-6 mb-2">2. The Southeast Corridor (Southeast Hubs)</h3>
<p><strong>Key states:</strong> Georgia, North Carolina, South Carolina, and Tennessee.</p>
<p>With the manufacturing boom in the South, cities like Atlanta (GA), Charlotte (NC), Greenville (SC), and
  Nashville (TN) have become highly profitable hubs. Atlanta is the primary freight artery for the South,
  guaranteeing very low wait times for reloads back toward the Midwest or Northeast.</p>

<h3 class="text-lg md:text-xl font-semibold text-white mt-6 mb-2">3. The Texas Border Hub (South Central
  Corridor)</h3>
<p><strong>Key points:</strong> Laredo, San Antonio, Dallas, and Houston.</p>
<p>Laredo (TX) is the most crucial inland port for trade between the US and Mexico. Cross-border industrial
  freight creates massive demand for rapid cargo van transportation northward. <strong>Heads up:</strong> Laredo
  can be a trap without established brokers, but staying within the Dallas-San Antonio-Houston triangle is
  highly profitable during peak seasons.</p>

<h3 class="text-lg md:text-xl font-semibold text-white mt-6 mb-2">4. The Great Lakes Region (Upper Great Lakes)</h3>
<p><strong>Key states:</strong> Wisconsin and Minnesota.</p>
<p>Cities like Milwaukee (WI) and Minneapolis (MN) host heavy concentrations of agricultural machinery, processed
  foods, and medical device manufacturing. Inbound rates are competitive, and outbound trips back to the Chicago
  corridor are short and highly profitable.</p>

<h3 class="text-lg md:text-xl font-semibold text-white mt-6 mb-2">5. The Mid-Atlantic Corridor</h3>
<p><strong>Key states:</strong> Pennsylvania, New Jersey, and Maryland.</p>
<p>Dense populations and major shipping ports like New York/New Jersey and Baltimore generate a steady flow of
  specialized air freight and distribution cargo. Cities like Philadelphia (PA), Allentown (PA), and Newark (NJ)
  are excellent for quick reloads, though you must factor high toll road costs into your Break-Even math.</p>
