Module 2 · Lesson 1

How to use the calculator step by step

9 min read Practice Free
What you will learn
  1. What goes in each calculator field.
  2. The difference between tolls (expense) and extra charges (income).
  3. What each button does and why you should save your loads.

01Before you start: your costs

The calculator decides with your costs. If Settings is empty or out of date, the verdict will be too. Once a month, check your fixed costs (vehicle payment, insurance, permits), your MPG, your maintenance per mile, your miles per month and your target margin.

About the examples in this module

The screenshots come from an example account with a cost of about $0.55 per mile and a 20% target margin. With your costs the numbers will be different, but you read them the same way.

02Step 1: the route

In Route information, type the Origin (where you pick up) and the Destination (where you deliver). Pick the suggestion from the list so Google calculates the exact miles. If the load has more deliveries, use Add stop.

Company and Load # are optional, but they help you find the load later in History.

03Step 2: the miles

Loaded milesFilled in automatically from the Google route, origin to destination
Empty milesThe ones you drive without freight: to the pickup and, if needed, to reposition after delivery
Total milesThe sum of both. RPM and costs are calculated with this number

In the example you are in Dallas: 33 empty miles to Fort Worth and 262 loaded to Houston. Total: 295 miles.

04Step 3: the money

Total rateWhat the broker pays for the load (include the fuel surcharge if it comes separately)
RPMIf you type the rate, RPM is calculated over total miles. You can also type the RPM and the app calculates the rate
TollsAn expense of this load: it is subtracted from your profit and added to the suggested price, at cost. If you do not know it, leave it at 0
Extra charges to customerExtra income: unloading, waiting time or detention paid separately

05Step 4: the panel and the buttons

The panel on the right (below on a phone) updates by itself. In the example it says ACCEPT, with $385.75 net profit, a 68.9% margin and about $386 per day. In lesson 3 you learn to read each part.

Accept and saveSaves the load to History. Your saved loads feed Zones, Finances and Lex
Copy priceCopies the total charge so you can paste it in your message to the broker
ClearClears the form for the next offer

06Practice in the app

Your turn

Open the calculator and enter the last offer you got: route, empty miles and rate. See which verdict it gives you before moving on to lesson 2.

In summary

  • Settings first: the calculator decides with your costs.
  • Total miles = loaded + empty; RPM is calculated with them.
  • Tolls is an expense; extra charges to customer is income.
  • Save your loads so Zones, Finances and Lex have real data.
