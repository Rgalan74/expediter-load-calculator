Module 2 · Lesson 3

Reading the result: accept, negotiate or reject

9 min read Practice Free
What you will learn
  1. The exact rules the panel uses for each verdict.
  2. Why a short load with a high RPM can come out EVALUATE.
  3. How to use Break-even, Target and Counter-offer to negotiate.

01How the app decides

The panel checks the load in this order. The first rule that applies gives the verdict:

REJECTRPM is below your cost per mile, or net profit is negative. You lose money.
EVALUATEA short load (150 miles or less) that leaves under $150, or profit per day under $100. Even if the RPM looks good.
ACCEPTRPM reaches your threshold: cost ÷ (1 − target margin), or your minimum RPM from Settings if that is higher.
EVALUATEEverything else: it covers your cost but does not reach your target.
In the example account

Cost of $0.55 and a 20% margin: the ACCEPT threshold is $0.55 ÷ 0.80 = $0.69 per mile. Below $0.55 is REJECT. In between, EVALUATE.

02Three rates for the same route

Detroit → Atlanta: 722 loaded miles and 30 empty, 752 in total.

03When a high RPM is not enough

Fort Worth → Dallas, 33 miles for $120. The RPM is $3.64, but the panel says EVALUATE: it is a short load that leaves $101.85, less than the $150 the app asks for on a load of 150 miles or less.

04If it says EVALUATE: negotiate with the suggested price

When the load does not reach ACCEPT, the Suggested price block gives you three numbers for that same route:

Break-evenYour cost × total miles. Below this you lose$414
TargetWhat you need for your margin$517
Counter-offerTarget + 8%: leaves room for the broker to come down$559

What to say

With empty miles

"The load works for me, but I have 30 empty miles to the pickup and it is 752 miles in total. To make it work I need $559. Can you do better?"

Into a bad zone

"After delivery I have to drive empty to find the next load. With those miles, my number is $559."

With extra stops

"It has two stops and that adds hours. I can do it for $559."

Always back it up with a concrete fact: miles, stops or zone. "I want more" convinces nobody.

05Before you say yes: Keep in mind

Below the suggested price, the panel can show warnings: weekend delivery, zones where outbound freight is hard to find, your saved notes for that place or a low profit per day. They do not change the verdict, but they tell you what to check.

The rule you do not break

Below Break-even you lose money. It only makes sense if that load gets you out of a bad zone and you know exactly what it costs you.

In summary

  • REJECT: RPM below your cost or negative profit.
  • ACCEPT: RPM at your threshold, cost ÷ (1 − margin) or your minimum RPM.
  • EVALUATE: in between, or a short load / low profit per day.
  • Negotiate with Counter-offer, accept from Target, never below Break-even.
