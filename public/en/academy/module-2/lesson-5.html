Module 2 · Lesson 5

Case study: two loads with the numbers

8 min read Practice Free
What you will learn
  1. How repositioning changes a load verdict.
  2. How to use the panel counter-offer on a real call.
  3. How much profit changes with a single negotiation.

01The case

Carlos is an example character: a cargo van driver based in Detroit, with a cost of $0.55 per mile and a 20% target margin, the same as the account in the screenshots. The miles are real Google miles and the figures come from the app panel.

02Monday: Detroit → Nashville

Pay$900
Miles20 empty + 533 loaded553 mi
RPM$1.63
Net profitAbout $596 per day · ACCEPT$595.85

A clear load. Carlos accepts it and saves it.

03Tuesday: the Laredo offer

In Nashville he is offered Laredo, TX: 1,094 miles for $850. If he only enters the route, the panel says ACCEPT with $248 profit.

But Carlos knows there is little freight for a van in Laredo. He will have to drive empty to San Antonio: 157 miles. He puts them in Empty miles:

Total miles1,251 mi
RPM$0.68
Net profit$161.95
Per dayEVALUATE~$54

Three days of work for $162. The suggested price shows Target $861 and Counter-offer $929.

04The negotiation

Carlos to the broker

"I am interested in the load, but from Laredo I have 157 empty miles to San Antonio to reload. To make it work I need $1,050."

The broker comes back with $1,000. Carlos enters it in the calculator before answering:

RPM$0.80
VerdictACCEPT
Net profitAbout $104 per day$311.95

He accepts.

05What changed

Without counting empty miles
Nashville$596

With the numbers
Nashville$596

Profit$908
+$150
with one call, on the same miles: the Laredo profit almost doubles

He did not have to drive more. He had to put every mile in the calculator and ask with a concrete fact.

06Your turn: two-week challenge

  • Week 1: run every offer you get through the calculator, including the ones you will reject.
  • Always enter empty miles and repositioning.
  • When it says EVALUATE, ask for the counter-offer with a concrete fact.
  • Week 2: compare your profit per day with the previous two weeks in Finances.

In summary

  • A load that looks good may not be once you add the empty miles after it.
  • The suggested price gives you one number to ask for and another to accept.
  • Check the broker counter-offer in the calculator before answering.
