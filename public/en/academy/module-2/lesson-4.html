Common Data Entry Mistakes (And How to Avoid Them)

📖 8 min read 🎓 Module 2 - Lesson 4

The 7 most expensive mistakes

Mistake #1: Ignoring repositioning miles

The mistake

Leaving the "Repositioning" field completely blank when you deliver to a dead zone.

The reality: Say a load goes to Laredo, TX. You WILL need to drive 280 empty miles back to San Antonio just to find the next load. Apparent RPM: $1.60/mile → REAL RPM: $1.25/mile. The impact: a $0.35/mile error = roughly $448 in "phantom profits" that don't exist.

The fix: ALWAYS ask yourself: "Is there freight exactly where I'm delivering?" If the answer is NO → add repositioning miles. Simple as that.

Mistake #2: Using incomplete, vague addresses

The mistake

Typing just "Chicago" (the metropolitan area is massive, easily spanning over 100 miles).

The reality: "Chicago" could mean Downtown, the O'Hare airport (15mi away), Joliet (40mi away), or Aurora (45mi away). The impact: a potential error of 40-80 miles.

The fix: Bad: "Chicago" — Better: "Chicago, IL" — Best: "1234 Main St, Chicago, IL 60601".

Mistake #3: Forgetting to update Current Location

The mistake

You were in Detroit on Monday, drove to Atlanta on Tuesday, but your calculator still thinks you're in Detroit.

The reality: Your entire deadhead calculation will be completely wrong.

The fix: Every single time you open the calculator, verify your location. Enable GPS auto-update in your Settings to take that off your plate.

Mistake #4: Leaving tolls and fees out of payment

The mistake

The broker says: "$1,200 base + $60 tolls" → you enter: "$1,200".

The reality: If the broker is reimbursing tolls or detentions, that is additional revenue hitting your account.

The fix: Add EVERYTHING together: Base $1,200 + FSC $150 + Tolls $60 + Detention $50 = $1,460 total income.

Mistake #5: Confusing "Loaded Miles" with "Total Miles"

The mistake

Broker says "it's an 800-mile run" → you assume that's total miles → you input it incorrectly.

The reality: Brokers almost ALWAYS quote loaded miles only. Your deadhead to the pickup is completely your problem.

The fix: 99% of the time, just leave the "Loaded Miles" field blank. Let the app automatically calculate the exact routing for you.

Mistake #6: Failing to save rejected loads

The mistake

You calculate a load → it looks terrible → you close the app (leaving no record that it ever happened).

The reality: Lex AI doesn't learn, and you lose valuable reference data for similar loads in the future.

The fix: ALWAYS hit save to History, even if you declined it. Lex will quickly learn your preferences and start pointing out trends.

Mistake #7: Using an outdated Cost Per Mile (CPM)

The mistake

You configured your CPM 6 months ago at $0.95/mile. Meanwhile, fuel skyrocketed and insurance went up → your actual CPM is now $1.15/mile.

The reality: The app will mistakenly tell you loads are "profitable" when you're actually losing cash.

The fix: Review your CPM monthly. Head to Settings → Cost Per Mile → Update.

The checklist: before calculating any load

Pre-calculation checklist
Is my current location correct (or auto-updated)?
Do I have the FULL pickup address (not just a city)?
Do I have the FULL delivery address?
Did I include ALL payment details (base + FSC + tolls + fees)?
Do I know if the delivery zone will require repositioning?
Is my CPM accurately updated for this month?
Did I clarify if the broker quote was "loaded" or "total" miles?

If every single box checks out → run the calculation with absolute confidence. If you're missing info → call the broker immediately and get it.

Garbage in = Garbage out.

The calculator app is flawless at crunching numbers... but only if you feed it flawless data. Avoid these → your business numbers will be 99% bulletproof.

Watch out for the 7 common slip-ups:

  1. Ignoring repositioning
  2. Vague addresses
  3. Outdated current locations
  4. Missing toll/fee reimbursements
  5. Confusing loaded miles with total miles
  6. Forgetting to save declined loads
  7. Relying on an outdated CPM
