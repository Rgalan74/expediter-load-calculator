🎯 THE ROUTE CLASSIFICATION SYSTEM

S-TIER 🟢🟢 (ELITE)

  • RPM > $1.70/mi consistently
  • Net margin > 40%
  • Profit/hour > $45
  • Easy, available backhaul
  • High frequency of available loads
  • ACTION: MAXIMIZE — Actively pursue these aggressively.

A-TIER 🟢 (PREFERRED)

  • RPM $1.50-1.70/mi
  • Net margin 35-40%
  • Profit/hour $35-45
  • Moderate, reliable backhaul
  • ACTION: PRIORITIZE — Always accept these without hesitation.

B-TIER 🟡 (ACCEPTABLE)

  • RPM $1.30-1.50/mi
  • Net margin 25-35%
  • Profit/hour $25-35
  • Variable, unreliable backhaul
  • ACTION: ACCEPT — Only if it fits into your circuit strategy.

C-TIER 🟠 (AVOID)

  • RPM $1.10-1.30/mi
  • Net margin 15-25%
  • Profit/hour $15-25
  • Difficult or non-existent backhaul
  • ACTION: AVOID — Only consider if financially desperate.

D-TIER 🔴 (REJECT)

  • RPM < $1.10/mi
  • Net margin < 15%
  • Profit/hour < $15
  • Virtually no backhaul options
  • ACTION: ALWAYS REJECT — Never accept these under any circumstances.

💡 THE CORE TAKEAWAY

Not all routes are created equal.
The secret is not working MORE hours.
It is working the RIGHT routes.

Route analysis consistently reveals:

  • 20% of your routes generate 60% of all profit (Tier S/A).
  • 50% of your routes are just "okay" (Tier B).
  • 30% of your routes are actively destroying profitability (Tier C/D).

The metrics that matter most, per route:

  1. Profit Per Hour (the #1 metric, always)
  2. Net Margin (measures business sustainability)
  3. RPM (measures rate efficiency)
  4. Backhaul Rate (measures load continuity)
  5. Load Frequency (measures operational consistency)

The Optimization Process:
WEEKS 1-2: Identify your unprofitable bad routes.
WEEKS 3-6: Test replacement routes strategically.
WEEKS 7-10: Gradually replace the bad with the good.
WEEKS 11-12: Evaluate your total financial results.

Typical real-world outcome:
+30-50% increase in monthly net profit

Eliminate 2-3 of your worst routes.
Maximize 2-3 of your best routes.
= Transform your business completely in 90 days.
