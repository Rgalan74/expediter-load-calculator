Gross Profit vs Real Profit (And the Time Factor)

📖 7 min read 🎓 Module 1 - Lesson 4

📌 THE THREE LAYERS OF PROFIT

Most operators only see the first one:

  1. 💰 Gross Profit = What you see in the offer
  2. 💵 Real Profit = What remains after ALL costs
  3. ⏰ Profit per Hour = Real profit ÷ Time invested

Only #3 tells you if it is actually worth it

📊 REAL CASE: The Load That "Paid Well"

Load Offer:

  • Origin: Dallas, TX
  • Destination: Chicago, IL
  • Pay: $1,200
  • Loaded miles: 900

❌ SURFACE-LEVEL ANALYSIS:

$1,200 ÷ 900 miles = $1.33/mile

"$1.33/mile! Good rate, I'll take it."

✅ LEVEL 1 ANALYSIS: Real Profit

REVENUE: Pay: $1,200

COSTS:

  • Deadhead (50 miles): 50 × $0.94 = $47
  • Loaded (900 miles): 900 × $0.94 = $846
  • Repositioning (100 miles): 100 × $0.94 = $94
  • Total costs: $987

REAL PROFIT: $1,200 - $987 = $213

"$213 profit, not bad..."

✅✅ LEVEL 2 ANALYSIS: Profit per Hour (THE TRUTH)

TIME INVESTED:

  • Deadhead drive: 1 hour
  • Loading time: 1.5 hours
  • Drive time (900 mi @ 60 mph): 15 hours
  • Unloading time: 1 hour
  • Mandatory breaks (HOS): 2 hours
  • Repositioning drive: 2 hours
  • TOTAL TIME: 22.5 hours

$213 ÷ 22.5 hours = $9.47/hour

🚨 BRUTAL REALITY: $9.47/hour is BELOW minimum wage

You worked almost a full day for less than a job at McDonald's

💡 COMPARISON WITH ANOTHER LOAD

Alternative Load:

  • Origin: Dallas, TX
  • Destination: Houston, TX
  • Pay: $400
  • Loaded miles: 250

Full analysis:

  • REVENUE: $400
  • COSTS: $263 (30 DH + 250 loaded + 0 repos)
  • REAL PROFIT: $137
  • TIME: 5.5 hours
  • PROFIT/HOUR: $24.91/hour ✅

📊 FINAL COMPARISON

LOAD A (Chicago) LOAD B (Houston)
Total pay $1,200 ✅ $400 ❌
Real profit $213 $137
Time invested 22.5 hrs 5.5 hrs
Profit/hour $9.47/hr ❌ $24.91/hr ✅
BEST STRATEGY: Run Load B TWICE (11 hours total)
  • Profit: $137 × 2 = $274
  • Time: 11 hours
  • Rate: $24.91/hour

vs. Load A once (22.5 hours):

  • Profit: $213
  • Time: 22.5 hours
  • Rate: $9.47/hour

DIFFERENCE: $61 MORE in HALF the time

🚀 STRATEGIES TO MAXIMIZE $/HOUR

1. Prioritize Turnaround Speed

Fast load (4-6 hrs) at $1.30/mile > Long load (18-20 hrs) at $1.40/mile

2. Avoid "Loading Hell"

Customers with 3+ hours of wait time — even if they pay $0.10-0.15 more — are NOT worth it

3. Favor Zones with Quick Reload

Zone B: Load pays $1.35, reload in 8 hrs > Zone A: Load pays $1.50, wait time 48 hrs

4. Consider "Short Premium Loads"

300-mile load at $1.60/mile in 5 hours = $36/hour
vs. 1,000-mile load at $1.40/mile in 18 hours = $22/hour

The "small" load pays 64% better per hour

💡 KEY TAKEAWAY

Total profit matters.
Real profit matters more.
Profit PER HOUR is what TRULY counts.

Think in TIME, not just in MONEY.

A load that pays "a lot" but takes "a long time"
can be WORSE than a small, fast load.
