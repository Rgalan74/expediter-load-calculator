Module 1 · Lesson 4

Gross profit, real profit and the time factor

7 min read Fundamentals Free
What you will learn
  1. The three layers: gross, real and per time.
  2. Why a load that pays more can leave you less per hour.
  3. Where to see your profit per day in the app.

01The three layers of profit

Gross profitWhat you see on the offer
Real profitWhat is left after all your costs
Profit per timeReal profit ÷ hours or days the load takes
The one that matters

Only the third one tells you whether the load is worth your time. A long load can leave you a lot of profit and still pay you little per hour.

02Case: the load that "paid well"

Dallas → Chicago for $1,200: 927 loaded miles, 50 empty to the pickup and 100 to reposition. Quick look: $1,200 ÷ 927 = $1.29 per mile. Looks good.

03The other option

Dallas → Houston for $400: 239 loaded miles and 30 empty. Costs: 269 × $0.534 = $144. Real profit: $256 in 6.5 hours (0.5 empty, 1 loading, 4 driving, 1 unloading).

04Profit per day and your minimum

For a van, the app counts 10 driving hours per day. With that it estimates your profit per day and warns you with "Low daily profit" when a load leaves less than $100 per day.

Set your own minimum

Think about what you need to make each month. If you want $4,000 clear working 22 days, you need about $182 per day. Compare every load with that number.

06How to make more per hour

  • Look for loads with fast loading and unloading. A 4 to 6-hour load at $1.30 per mile can beat an 18 to 20-hour load at $1.40.
  • Avoid long waits. A customer who makes you wait 3 hours or more is not worth it even if they pay $0.10 or $0.15 more per mile.
  • Prefer zones where you reload fast. $1.35 per mile with a reload in 8 hours usually beats $1.50 with a 48-hour wait.
  • Consider well-paid short loads. 300 miles at $1.60 in 6 hours leaves about $48 per hour; 1,000 miles at $1.40 in 19 hours, about $40.

07In the app

In summary

  • Gross profit misleads; real profit subtracts all your costs.
  • Profit per hour or per day tells you whether the load is worth your time.
  • Set your minimum per day and compare every load with it.
