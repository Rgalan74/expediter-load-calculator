Module 1 · Lesson 2

Visible costs vs hidden costs

7 min read Fundamentals Free
What you will learn
  1. Which costs you see every day and which ones slip by.
  2. Why your cost per mile changes with the miles you run each month.
  3. How to calculate your real cost per mile and put it in the app.

01The problem

Most drivers count fuel and tolls, because that is what they pay with the card every day. But the van payment, insurance, wear and tear and lodging on the road also come out of every load, even if you don't see them at the time.

The common mistake

Pricing a load thinking only about fuel. A load that looks profitable may not cover the rest of your costs.

What about food?

You would pay for food at home anyway, so it is a personal expense: record it in Finances, but do not add it to your cost per mile. Lodging does count, because you only pay it when a load leaves you away from home. If you sleep in the van, your lodging is $0.

Fuel, the one everybody watches, is just over a third of the real cost. If you price thinking of $0.20 a mile, you are actually spending $0.53.

Watch out

Running more miles lowers your cost per mile only if they are miles that pay. Empty miles also burn fuel and wear the van.

04Depreciation

It is the cost you don't see until you sell the van. If you buy it for $55,000 and in 5 years it is worth $25,000, it lost $30,000 in 60 months: $500 a month. At 8,000 miles a month that is $0.063 per mile.

Tip

If your van is paid off, the payment goes away but depreciation doesn't. Add it to the Other Costs field in Settings. If you are still paying for it, the monthly payment already covers a good part of that loss: don't count it twice.

06Warning signs

Guideline values for a gas cargo van:

  • Fuel above $0.25 per mile: check your MPG (tire pressure, speed, idling at stops).
  • Maintenance, tires and repairs above $0.10 per mile: your van needs a check-up.
  • Fixed costs above $0.35 per mile: you need more miles that pay or lower payments.
  • Total cost close to your average RPM: you have no margin left; every weak load costs you money.

In summary

  • Fuel is only part of your cost; in the example, just over a third.
  • Fixed costs are spread over your miles: with fewer miles, each mile costs more.
  • Your key number is your real cost per mile. Keep it: you will use it in the break-even lesson.
