Module 1 · Lesson 5

Why a "paid load" is not a "profitable load"

8 min read Fundamentals Free
What you will learn
  1. Why the destination matters as much as the pay.
  2. How to compare loads by their full cycle and profit per day.
  3. What the app tells you about zones and timing.

01The mindset shift

Driver

"I got paid, I am good."

Operator

"Did this load improve my position for the next one?"

About the numbers

The waits and return rates are examples. What matters is the method: look at the load, the wait and the return together. You see your real data in Zones.

03The right questions

Before "how much does it pay?" and "what is the RPM?", ask "where does this load leave me?".

The destination

  • Is there good outbound freight in that zone?
  • How long have I waited there before?
  • Do outbound loads pay well or will I have to reposition?

The timing

  • Do I deliver on a good day and at a good time to reload?
  • Will I get stuck over the weekend?
  • Is it a good season in that zone?

The full cycle

  • This load plus the likely return: are they better than two short loads?
  • Does my profit per day reach my minimum?
  • Am I thinking about the week or just this load?

04Decision matrix

Pays well + good zoneE.g. $1,500 to AtlantaAccept
Pays well + bad zoneE.g. $1,500 to LaredoAnalyze
Pays little + good zoneE.g. $800 to ChicagoConsider
Pays little + bad zoneE.g. $800 to MontanaReject

05Think in weeks, not loads

Rookie

"I made $300 on this load today. Good day."

Professional

"This week: $2,100 in 5 days, $420 per day. Last week: $1,800 in 6 days, $300 per day. I improved 40% by choosing zones better."

06In the app

Apply it in the app

In Zones you see your RPM and profit by destination state based on your history, and the app learns which zones have been hard for you to leave. On each load panel, Keep in mind warns you about weekends, those hard zones and your notes about the place. In Finances you see your profit month by month.

In summary

  • A load is measured by its destination, wait and return, not just its pay.
  • Compare by profit per day and think about the whole week.
  • Use Zones and "Keep in mind" before accepting.

Module 1 complete

You now know how to calculate your real RPM, your real cost per mile, your break-even and your profit per day, and how to think about each load as part of your week.
