Red Flags and When to Say NO

📖 11 min read 🎓 Module 4 - Lesson 4 🚨 Protection

Why this matters

Not all brokers are good people. Not all freight loads are worth it. Some loads are not worth the cash they pay.

Knowing exactly when to confidently say "no" means you:

  • Protect your business license
  • Protect your valuable time
  • Protect your hard-earned reputation
  • Protect your daily mental health

Red flags level 1: caution required

🟡 Flag #1: Extremely vague load details
You: "What is the exact pickup city?"
Broker: "Somewhere in the greater Chicago area"

You: "What is the specific delivery window?"
Broker: "Flexible, sometime this week"

You: "What is the actual commodity?"
Broker: "Just general freight, no big deal"

Why it's a major problem:

  • "Chicago area" could mean a 40-mile difference
  • "Flexible" often means the client doesn't care (low priority = low pay)
  • "General freight" could be heavy, oversized, or even hazardous
  • Vagueness = an amateur broker or one hiding a bad load

Your action: Proceed with caution. Demand specifics before accepting. Get everything in writing. If they remain vague, decline.

🟡 Flag #2: The rate keeps mysteriously changing
Monday: "The load pays exactly $1,200"
Tuesday: "So actually, it's $1,150 now"
Wednesday: "The customer just said $1,100 is their hard max"

Why it's a major problem:

  • The broker holds no authority over the client
  • Or they are testing your financial desperation
  • Or they are genuinely disorganized (a red flag in itself)
  • Your time is being wasted

Your action: Set a hard boundary: "I need a firm rate to commit. Call me back when you have the final number." If the games continue, decline and move on.

🟡 Flag #3: Pressuring you for an immediate decision
Broker: "I need an answer RIGHT NOW or I'm moving
on to the next carrier."
[You haven't had time to calculate your costs]

Why it's suspicious:

  • Legitimate urgency exists, but...
  • ...legitimate brokers will give you 15-20 mins
  • Pressure = trying to bypass your judgment
  • It often hides a problem with the load

Your action: Call the bluff: "I need 15 minutes to run my numbers. If you need to move on, I understand. But I don't commit blindly." Real urgency? They'll wait. Fake urgency? They'll disappear (good riddance).

Red flags level 2: imminent danger

🔴 Flag #4: Asking you for deposits or "fees"
Broker: "There is a standard $200 onboarding setup fee"
Broker: "I need a $150 insurance deposit up front"
Broker: "Pay me $100 for the load assignment fee"

This is a scam.

The reality:

  • Legitimate brokers never charge carriers
  • You provide the service; they pay you
  • Any request for an upfront "fee" = a guaranteed scam
  • They will take your money and vanish

Your action: Decline instantly. Block their number. Report them to the FMCSA if you can. Warn other carriers.

🔴 Flag #5: The broker cannot be verified by MC number
You: "What is your official MC number?"
Broker: "I don't have it handy right now"
Broker: "We are a brand new company"
Broker: "Just look us up online, we're legit"

Verification protocol:

  • Go to the FMCSA SAFER website
  • Look up the MC number
  • Verify: Active status, authority, and insurance
  • Check their broker bond (must be $75K minimum)

Your action: If you cannot verify them, decline. No exceptions. Ever.

🔴 Flag #6: Refusal to send a written rate confirmation
You: "Can you email over the written rate confirmation?"
Broker: "Verbal agreement is fine"
Broker: "I always send it after the delivery is complete"
Broker: "Our system is currently down"

Why this is critical:

  • Verbal agreements are legally unenforceable
  • The broker can change the rate later
  • You have no proof of the agreement
  • It is a recipe for a payment dispute

Your action: No written rate con = no load moves. "I require written confirmation before dispatch. Company policy, no exceptions." If they refuse, they are planning to rip you off.

🔴 Flag #7: Payment terms beyond 30 days

The industry standard:

15-30 daysNormal
30-45 daysAcceptable if premium rate
45+ daysWarning flag
60+ daysDanger zone

Your action: Decline unless the rate is a +25-30% premium, the broker has a flawless credit history, you can afford to wait, or you use a factoring company (cash advance).

Physical freight red flags

🟡 Freight flag #1: "The customer doesn't know you're coming"

Example: You arrive at pickup. Customer: "We never got a pickup notice." Customer: "We have nothing ready for you."

The cost to you:

  • 2-4 hours wasted
  • Potential detention pay battles (often $0)
  • Losing out on other load opportunities

Prevention: Before you launch: "Can you confirm the client is expecting me at [time]? Do they have my contact info?" If there is hesitation, force the broker to call the client while you wait.

🟡 Freight flag #2: Undisclosed multiple stops

Example: Rate con says: "Detroit to Nashville." At the pickup dock: "Oh yeah, you have 3 stops along the way."

The impact:

  • Every stop = +1-2 hours of delays
  • More loading/unloading labor
  • The same pay for 4x the work

Your action: Renegotiate on the spot: "The rate confirmation states 1 stop. For 3 additional stops, I require $200 more before I depart."

🔴 Freight flag #3: The commodity does not match

Extreme safety and liability hazard.

Example: Rate con says: "General auto parts." At the pickup dock: it is hazardous chemicals.

Why this is serious:

  • Hazardous materials require special federal certification
  • Your insurance coverage is invalid for this
  • It is illegal to transport without compliance
  • Massive federal fines ($75K+) if caught
  • You could permanently lose your operating authority

Your action: Refuse to load. "This was not disclosed as HAZMAT. I am not certified or equipped. I am rejecting this load." Your authority > their profit margin.

How to professionally say no

Script #1: The soft decline (keep the relationship open)

Situation: The rate is too low, but you want to work with this broker again.

"Hey [Name], I really appreciate you thinking of me for this run. Unfortunately, my numbers just don't work at that rate for this particular lane.

But I definitely want to keep working together. Please keep me in mind for your [route] loads or anything landing in the $1.40-1.70/mile range.

I'll always answer when you call."

Tone goal: Grateful, specific about future needs, positive.

Script #2: The firm decline (protect your business)

Situation: There is a valid red flag issue, and you need to decline firmly.

"I really appreciate the offer, but I'm going to have to pass on this one.

[Optional: brief, professional reason]
'The payment terms don't work for my business' /
'I require a written rate confirmation before dispatching' /
'The load details aren't clear enough for me to commit.'

Thanks for understanding."

Tone goal: Polite but unyielding, leaving no room for negotiation.

Script #3: The hard no (burn the bridge if necessary)

Situation: It is a scam, illegal, or a physically dangerous situation.

"I will not be able to work with you on this.

[If scam]: This appears fraudulent.
[If illegal]: This violates FMCSA regulations.
[If unethical]: This does not align with my operating standards.

Please do not contact me again."

[Block the number if necessary]

Tone goal: Direct, final, with no space for discussion.

The gut-check framework

When evaluating a questionable load, ask yourself:

  1. "Would I confidently tell my wife/parent I am taking this?" — If no, decline it.
  2. "Am I only considering this because I'm desperate today?" — If yes, decline it.
  3. "Would I take this exact load if it paid 2x the rate?" — If no, the problem isn't the money, it's the load itself.
  4. "Will I regret taking this tomorrow morning?" — If maybe, decline it.
  5. "Is my hesitation just normal caution, or a genuine concern?" — If genuine concern, decline it.
The rule

If 2 or more of these raise red flags, you must decline.

Saying "no" is a business superpower.

Every single bad load you decline:

  • Shields your business from losses
  • Saves your valuable time
  • Protects your operating license
  • Preserves your mental sanity

Red flags exist in distinct tiers:

🟡 YellowCaution required, demand specifics
🔴 RedImminent danger, likely decline
🚨 EmergencyRun away immediately

The declination framework:

  1. Verify the MC number (non-negotiable)
  2. Secure a written rate con (non-negotiable)
  3. Verify fair payment terms (≤30 days standard)
  4. Run the 5-question gut-check
  5. When in doubt, decline
Better to
  • Turn down 10 questionable loads
  • Secure 1 excellent load
Than to
  • Accept 10 questionable loads
  • Get ripped off on 3-4 of them

Your ability to say "no" is the armor of your business. Deploy it with confidence.

In the expediter game, your reputation is everything — defend it by rejecting freight that fails to meet your standards.
