Module 3 · Lesson 5

Thinking two or three loads ahead

8 min read Strategy Free
What you will learn
  1. Why the first load decides your week.
  2. How to compare two chains by profit per day.
  3. The four questions before accepting.

01A load is a move, not the game

The first load of the week decides where you will be for the rest of the week. That is why you should look at the whole chain: this load, the next one and the one back home.

The Laredo load pays $800 more than the Atlanta one, but over the whole week both chains leave almost the same per day. And chain 2 gets you home a day earlier, without depending on finding freight in Laredo.

The hidden risk

Chain 1 assumes you get a load out of Laredo fast. If you wait one more day, it drops to about $226 per day. Chain 2 only goes through green states.

03The four questions before accepting

  • Where does this load leave me? Check the state color in Zones.
  • What leaves from there and at what rate? Check your state table and the hard-to-leave warning.
  • How many empty miles to the next one? Add them in the calculator.
  • How much does the chain leave per day? Compare per day, not per load.

04Sacrifice moves

Sometimes it pays to accept a load that leaves little because it puts you in your circuit. A Louisville to Detroit load (363 miles) that barely clears your threshold can be worth more than a better paid one that takes you away from home. The condition: the next load from there has to make up for it.

Rule of thumb

Accept a weak load only if it moves you to a green state and you know which loads leave from there. Never below your Break-even.

In summary

  • Compare whole chains, not single loads.
  • A load that pays more can leave the same per day with more risk.
  • Accept a weak load only if it takes you to your circuit.

Module 3 complete

You now know what makes a zone profitable, how to read Zones, when to charge more to go in, when empty miles pay off and how to think about the whole week.
