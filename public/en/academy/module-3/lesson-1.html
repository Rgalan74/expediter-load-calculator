Module 3 · Lesson 1

What a profitable zone is (and how to find yours)

7 min read Strategy Free
What you will learn
  1. The four factors that make a zone profitable.
  2. How the app colors each state with your loads.
  3. What your home circuit is.

01What makes a zone profitable

A profitable zone is not the one that pays best to get in. It is the one that lets you get out fast with a good load. Four things decide it:

Outbound freightHow many loads for your vehicle type leave from there
Outbound rateHow much those loads pay per mile
WaitingHow many hours you sit until you get the next one
Empty milesHow many miles you drive without freight to the next pickup

02Your zones, not someone else's

"Best states" lists are of little use. Your vehicle, your base, your brokers and your schedule change the result. The most reliable source is your own loads: that is why you should save all of them with Accept and save.

03How the app classifies them

The Zones tab colors each state by the average RPM of the loads you took leaving that state, compared with your own numbers:

GreenRPM equal to or above your ACCEPT threshold (in the example account, $0.69)
YellowIt covers your cost but does not reach the threshold ($0.55 to $0.69)
RedBelow your cost per mile
Fewer than 3 loads

If a state has fewer than 3 loads, the app drops it one level: green to yellow, yellow to red. With one or two loads there is not enough information yet.

04Your home circuit

Over time you will see 3 or 4 green states connected to each other. That is your circuit: zones you get into, get out of fast and that bring you back near home. In the example, Michigan, Ohio and Georgia.

  • Save all your loads for 90 days.
  • Every month, check which states are green and which ones dropped.
  • Look for loads that leave you inside your circuit.

In summary

  • A zone is profitable if you get out fast with a good load.
  • Zones colors each state with the RPM of your outbound loads.
  • With fewer than 3 loads, the state drops one level.
